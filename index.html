Olugbenga Asaolu is an epidemiologist who works on infectious disease surveillance, outbreak intelligence and the analytical systems that help institutions act on evidence.

His career runs from programme monitoring and evaluation in Nigeria to epidemiological and data science work at the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Across that range, one question recurs: how does a public health institution turn data into a decision it can defend?

He combines epidemiological judgement with advanced analytics, reproducible methods and an understanding of how health systems actually operate. He is also committed to building the analytical capacity of the next generation of public health practitioners.

Read the full professional biography

Foundations in programme evaluation

Dr Asaolu began in public health practice in Nigeria, where he served as Senior Monitoring and Evaluation Officer at the Association for Reproductive and Family Health in Ibadan. In 2017 he took part in the Training Course in Sexual and Reproductive Health Research offered by the Geneva Foundation for Medical Education and Research. That period shaped a lasting emphasis on measurement that supports programme decisions rather than reporting alone.

Epidemiology and surveillance

He is an epidemiologist with professional experience at the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention in Atlanta. His interests centre on infectious disease surveillance, epidemic intelligence and the evaluation of surveillance systems, with a consistent attention to data quality and to the usefulness of an analysis to the person who must act on it.

Data science for decision support

Dr Asaolu builds reproducible analytical workflows in R and Python and has published open interactive analyses of public health data. He treats these tools as a means of making evidence transparent and usable, not as an end in themselves.

Scientific leadership and capacity

He is a Fellow of the Royal Society for Public Health and contributes to Strategic Insight Analytics, an initiative to strengthen applied analytical skills among public health professionals. He contributes to the scientific literature and to professional learning in applied epidemiology and data analysis.

Leadership & Impact

Four areas of professional focus, each organised around the same aim: evidence that institutions can trust and act on.

Epidemic Intelligence & Global Health Security

Outbreaks are decided by how quickly signals become understanding. The work here concerns surveillance intelligence, outbreak investigation and preparedness, with analysis designed to support timely, proportionate response.

Public Health Systems & Institutional Strengthening

Strong systems are measured, not assumed. This area covers the evaluation of surveillance performance, the quality of health information infrastructure and the technical capacity that keeps both dependable.

Data Science & Strategic Decision Intelligence

Advanced epidemiological analytics, reproducible research and decision-support tools, applied so that leaders receive clear answers with the uncertainty stated plainly.

Scientific Leadership & Capacity Development

Research contributions, manuscript development, professional education and mentoring. Durable capability comes from people who can reproduce, question and extend the analysis.

Reading the global HIV and AIDS record

Challenge
Decades of country-level HIV and AIDS data are hard to compare, which makes long-term trends difficult for decision-makers to see.
Role
Author of the analysis.
Approach
A reproducible analysis across countries and years, published openly with its methods.
Output
A public, interactive analysis that readers can examine and verify.

Burden and risk indicators in one view

Challenge
Prostate cancer mortality and population body-mass index are reported separately, so their long-run relationship is rarely examined together.
Role
Author of the dashboard.
Approach
An interactive dashboard that brings both indicators together over time and by country.
Output
A public dashboard summarising mortality and BMI trends from 1980 to 2008.

Strategic Insight Analytics

Challenge

Role
